HDB to condo upgrade: the 6-step plan

Most families I meet start by looking at showflats. I'd start with the maths. Here is the order I walk every upgrader through, with real numbers.

By Louis Tay, Huttons Asia, 16 years in property. Updated 21 November 2026. 9 min read.

1. Check you can buy

You can only buy private property once your flat has met its minimum occupation period (MOP). For most flats that is 5 years from the day you got the keys. Flats sold under the newer Plus and Prime models carry a 10-year MOP, so check your own flat's terms on the HDB website.

After MOP you can buy a condo and keep the flat. Owning two homes brings ABSD into play, which step 4 covers.

2. Work out your real cash

Your sale price is not your cash. Three things come off the top before you see a cent:

  1. The rest of your home loan, paid to the bank or HDB.
  2. Your CPF refund. Every dollar of CPF you used for the flat goes back into your Ordinary Account, plus 2.5% a year in accrued interest.
  3. Selling costs, such as agent and legal fees.

Here is a typical case: a flat that sells for S$650,000 with S$150,000 of loan left, and S$200,000 of CPF used over 10 years.

CPF refund: S$200,000 plus about S$56,000 interest− S$256,000

The good news: the S$256,000 going back to CPF is still yours. You can use it again for the next downpayment and stamp duty. The CPF accrued interest guide explains how the refund works.

3. Know your loan limit

Banks lend on two tests, and the lower one wins.

  • Loan-to-value (LTV). For your first housing loan, a bank lends up to 75% of the price. At least 5% must be paid in cash. The other 20% can be cash or CPF.
  • Total debt servicing ratio (TDSR). All your monthly loan payments together can't go over 55% of your gross monthly income. Banks test this at a higher interest rate than you'll pay. I use 4% a year.

To get the full 75%, the loan has to end by age 65 and run no more than 30 years for private property. Older buyers often get a shorter tenure, which cuts the loan.

A couple earning S$12,000 a month with no other loans can borrow roughly S$1.38 million on the TDSR test over 30 years. The cash test often bites first. The calculator shows which one limits you.

4. Sell first or buy first

This is the biggest decision in the whole plan, and the one people rush.

  • Sell first: no ABSD, and you know your cash exactly. You need somewhere to stay between homes.
  • Buy first: you move once. A married couple pays 20% ABSD upfront and gets it back only if they sell the flat in time.

I've written a full comparison with the timelines: sell first or buy first when upgrading from HDB.

5. New launch or resale condo

A new launch is paid in stages while it's being built. You pay 5% when you book and the rest of the 20% downpayment when you sign the sale and purchase agreement. The loan is drawn down as construction moves along, so early instalments are small.

A resale condo is paid in full at completion, usually 8 to 12 weeks after you exercise the option. You move in sooner and see exactly what you're buying.

For buy-first couples, a new launch has one more draw. The ABSD clock runs from the project's TOP, so you can live in your flat for the years it takes to build. The catch is that the 20% ABSD sits with IRAS for that whole time.

6. Count the costs people forget

  • Buyer's stamp duty: about S$49,600 on a S$1.6 million condo, payable within 14 days of signing.
  • Legal fees on both the sale and the purchase.
  • Renovation and furniture for the new place.
  • Rent if you sell first and the new home isn't ready.
  • Interest during the gap if you buy first and service two loans.

Questions people ask me

How long after MOP can I buy a condo?

Straight away. Once your flat has met its MOP, you can buy private property and keep the flat. If you still own the flat when you buy, ABSD applies on the condo. Married couples can claim it back if they sell the flat within the time IRAS allows.

How much cash do I need to upgrade from HDB to a condo?

At least 5% of the condo price in cash, plus up to 20% more in cash or CPF, plus buyer's stamp duty. On a S$1.6 million condo that is S$80,000 cash, S$320,000 cash or CPF, and about S$49,600 in stamp duty. Legal fees and renovation come on top.

Can I use my CPF refund for the new condo?

Yes. The CPF refund from your flat sale goes back into your Ordinary Account. You can use it for the condo downpayment, stamp duty and monthly instalments, within CPF's usage limits for private property.

Is it better to upgrade to a new launch or a resale condo?

It depends on your timeline and cash. A new launch is paid in stages and gives buy-first couples time to sell their flat. A resale condo lets you move in within months and see the actual unit. Many of my clients compare both before deciding.

What income do I need to upgrade from HDB?

It depends on the price, your age and your other loans. Banks cap all loan payments at 55% of gross income, tested at a higher rate. Put your figures into the HDB upgrade calculator on this site for a quick estimate.
