CPF accrued interest: how much cash will your HDB sale leave you?

Every month you pay your flat with CPF, a refund grows in the background. Most sellers only see it at the end. Here is how to see it now.

By Louis Tay, Huttons Asia, 16 years in property. Updated 21 November 2026. 6 min read.

What accrued interest is

When you use CPF for your flat, the money leaves your Ordinary Account. CPF keeps track of the interest it would have earned there, at 2.5% a year. When you sell, the principal you used plus that interest goes back into your CPF account. That total is your CPF refund.

Any housing grant you received is refunded the same way, with interest.

The refund isn't a fee. It goes back to your own CPF, ready for your next home. It does mean less cash in hand.

Worked examples

Take S$200,000 of CPF used. Here is roughly what you'd refund at sale. The interest is my estimate, compounded yearly.

Years since purchaseAccrued interestTotal CPF refund
5 yearsabout S$26,300about S$226,300
10 yearsabout S$56,000about S$256,000
15 yearsabout S$89,700about S$289,700

In real life the CPF you used builds up month by month, so your figure will differ. The point stands: the longer you've owned, the more of your sale price heads back to CPF.

When the sale doesn't cover it

Sometimes the sale price, minus the loan, is less than the CPF refund. People call this a negative sale. CPF takes back what the proceeds can cover and you don't top up the gap in cash. You do walk away with no cash from the sale.

For an upgrader, this changes the plan: your next downpayment has to come from savings and your CPF balance.

Check your exact figure

  1. Log in to the CPF website with Singpass.
  2. Go to your home ownership details and open the property.
  3. Note the principal withdrawn and the accrued interest for each owner.
  4. Add them up for all owners. That is your CPF refund.

Then put the number into the HDB upgrade calculator in place of the estimate.

Should you refund early?

CPF lets you make a voluntary housing refund before you sell. It puts cash back into your CPF now, where it earns interest for you. It doesn't add to your cash at sale, so I only suggest it to clients with spare cash and no near-term plan to upgrade.

Questions people ask me

Do I lose my CPF accrued interest when I sell my HDB?

No. The principal and accrued interest go back into your own CPF account. You can use them again for your next home, or keep them for retirement. What you lose is that part of the sale price as cash in hand.

What rate is CPF accrued interest?

2.5% a year, the Ordinary Account rate, on the CPF you used for the property. My examples compound it yearly as an estimate. Your CPF account shows the exact figure.

Do I need to refund housing grants when I sell my HDB?

Yes. CPF housing grants you received for the flat are refunded to your CPF account, with accrued interest, when you sell.

What happens if my HDB sale price is too low to cover the CPF refund?

CPF takes back what the sale proceeds can cover after the loan is repaid. You don't have to top up the gap in cash, but you'll receive no cash from the sale.

Can I reduce my CPF accrued interest?

A voluntary housing refund puts money back into CPF earlier, which slows how fast the accrued interest grows. It doesn't increase the cash you receive at sale, so it suits people with spare cash who aren't upgrading soon.
